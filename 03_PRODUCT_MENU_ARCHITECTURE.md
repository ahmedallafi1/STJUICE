# ST. JUICE — Product and Menu Architecture

## Objective

Offer the breadth customers expect from Juice Time and the clarity they expect from Chocolate DIP without presenting an unmanageable wall of products.

## Navigation model

Customers should be able to discover products through four parallel paths:

1. **Type** — juice, smoothie, crepe, waffle, fruit, chocolate or box.
2. **Mood** — Fresh, Fruity, Chocolate, Crunchy, Protein or Shareable.
3. **Occasion** — Morning, Study Night, Date Night, Game Day, Birthday, Office or Party.
4. **Account experience** — Guest, Regular, Student or Business/Catering.

## Menu-size control

- Feature only four to six best sellers on the home page.
- Keep approximately 35–45 primary launch products visible across categories.
- Generate variation through sizes and modifiers rather than dozens of duplicate product listings.
- Keep two or three limited drops active.
- Archive expired drops instead of leaving them out of stock indefinitely.

## Pricing ladder — temporary planning framework

Exact prices are not yet approved. The working architecture is:

- Entry products and shots.
- Core drinks.
- Premium signatures.
- Individual desserts.
- Loaded or limited products.
- Flights and twins.
- Party boxes and catering.

Direct ordering should offer better value than third-party delivery where lawful and operationally appropriate. Any price difference must be transparent.

## Product-page data requirements

Every product record must support:

- Name.
- Appetite description.
- Category and mood.
- Price and size.
- Availability by location and daypart.
- Images and optional video.
- Ingredients.
- Allergens.
- Verified nutrition when available.
- Modifiers and modifier prices.
- Preparation time.
- Delivery suitability.
- Badges.
- Rewards points.
- Frequently bought together.
- Alternatives when unavailable.

## Nutrition and allergen policy

- The interface will be designed in advance for calories, protein, ingredients and allergens.
- Exact numbers must not be invented.
- Public claims require verified recipe quantities, supplier specifications and portion sizes.
- Possible cross-contact must be disclosed.
- Halal, vegan, organic, gluten-free or Belgian-chocolate claims require operational confirmation.
